# W22 Sound and an Absolute Pointer  (M22)  — status: in progress
Goal: Burrow makes sound and follows the host's mouse without grabbing it. Demo: Doom plays its sound effects through an AC'97 card, recorded by QEMU into a WAV file that the test checks; `run /bin/tone` plays an A (440 Hz) that a host script measures; the pointer in X, Qt and paint sits exactly where the host's pointer is, with no click into the window and no Ctrl-Alt-G (PIT-32 closed); `make sound-smoke` and `make tablet-smoke` pass; `make test` green. Stories: E18.F05-F07 ([BACKLOG.md](../BACKLOG.md)).

Where we start (after W21): input is the PS/2 keyboard and mouse (`drivers/ps2`, IRQ 1 and 12), which feed `kernel/evdev` (`/dev/input/event0` keyboard, `event1` mouse, `mice` in mousedev's PS/2 stream format). QEMU sends PS/2 motion only while its window has grabbed the pointer, and only relative motion, so X and Qt accelerate and drift from the host's pointer (PIT-32). There is no sound at all: no driver, no `/dev/dsp`, and Doom is built without `FEATURE_SOUND` (doomgeneric's only sound module is SDL's). Go drivers have what a bus-master PCI device needs: `pci.Scan` and BARs, port I/O, `MapMMIO`, `DMAPage` (one zeroed 4 KiB page a device may read and write), and interrupts as wake-ups (`driver.Serve`, ADR-0005). The e1000 is the one bus-master device so far. Interrupt lines are the 8259's, and the nucleus lets one thread wait on a line (`irq_wait` answers `-EBUSY` to a second): PCI devices that share a line (QEMU's pc machine routes four PCI links onto IRQs 5, 9, 10 and 11) cannot both be served today. The smoke harness moves the mouse with the monitor's `mouse_move` (relative) and dumps the screen; it cannot hear anything.

Design (mechanism where it already is, drivers and policy in Go):
- **Shared interrupt lines first.** One goroutine serves a line and calls every driver registered on it (each checks its own device's status register and says whether it was the one); the nucleus is unchanged, its one waiter is the line's goroutine. The e1000 moves onto it, so a second PCI device on its line just works.
- **AC'97 for sound.** QEMU's `-device AC97` (Intel ICH, the most documented PCI audio): the mixer (NAM) and the bus master (NABM) are two I/O BARs; output is the PCM-out channel, a buffer descriptor list of 32 entries pointing at DMA pages, the last valid index moved forward as data arrives, an interrupt on each buffer completed. Variable rate audio (VRA, which QEMU offers) sets the sample rate; 16-bit stereo little-endian is the card's format, and the driver converts what it is given.
- **`/dev/dsp`, OSS's interface.** The device file Linux's OSS emulation offers and small programs (and SDL's `dsp` driver) still use: `write` of PCM samples, blocking while the ring is full (and `O_NONBLOCK`, `poll` for `POLLOUT`), and the ioctls that set it up (`SNDCTL_DSP_SPEED`, `SETFMT` for `AFMT_S16_LE` and `AFMT_U8`, `CHANNELS`/`STEREO`, `GETOSPACE`, `GETODELAY`, `GETBLKSIZE`, `SYNC`, `RESET`). One opener at a time (`EBUSY`), as OSS without a mixer daemon.
- **Doom gets a sound module.** doomgeneric's engine calls a `sound_module_t`; Burrow's (`user/doom/i_burrowsound.c`) mixes up to eight channels of Doom's 8-bit 11025 Hz effects with their volume and separation into 16-bit stereo and writes them to `/dev/dsp` from a mixing thread. Music (MIDI through an OPL emulator) is not in this wave.
- **virtio for the pointer.** QEMU's `-device virtio-tablet-pci` is a virtio 1.0 PCI device whose events are already Linux input events (`type`, `code`, `value`; absolute X and Y in 0-32767). The transport (`kernel/virtio`: the PCI capabilities for the common, notify, ISR and device configuration, feature negotiation, split virtqueues in DMA pages) is pure Go tested against a fake device, and is what virtio-blk and virtio-net will use later.
- **An absolute pointer is an evdev device.** `/dev/input/event2` reports `EV_ABS` (`ABS_X`, `ABS_Y`, with `EVIOCGABS` ranges) and the buttons; `/dev/input/mice` turns absolute positions into exact relative moves against the screen's size, as Linux's mousedev does for a tablet, so programs reading the PS/2 stream follow too. X's pointer acceleration would multiply those moves, so `startx` switches it off; Qt reads the tablet directly (its evdev mouse handler takes absolute axes).
- **Tests hear and point.** QEMU's `-audiodev wav` writes what the card played to a file; `tests/smoke/checkwav.py` measures its frequency (zero crossings) and length, or its energy after a moment the keys file marks. The harness gains `abs X Y` (QMP's `input-send-event` with absolute axes, on a second monitor socket), so a test can put the pointer on a pixel whatever happened before.

### [x] W22-S01 Shared interrupt lines (E18.F05)
`kernel/driver`: a line registry, `ServeShared(line, name, handler func() bool)`: the first driver on a line starts its goroutine (wait, call every handler, ack), later ones join it; a handler returns whether its device raised the interrupt, and a line that nobody claims is counted and logged once (a stuck line must not spin silently). The e1000 moves to it (its interrupt cause register already says). Host tests with `haltest.FakeIRQ`: two fake devices on one line, each served once per interrupt it raised, in any order, under `-race`; an unclaimed interrupt is counted. On Burrow: `make net-smoke` unchanged; a marker lists the lines and who shares them (`[driver] INFO: irq 11: e1000`). QEMU's routing is checked rather than assumed: the PCI header's interrupt line is what the driver uses.
As built: `kernel/driver/shared.go`: `Lines` (made by `NewLines(irq, log)`; `driver.Env.Lines`), `Serve(line, name, Handler)` where a `Handler` returns whether its device raised the interrupt; the first handler on a line starts the line's goroutine (`driver.Serve`, one waiter for the nucleus), later ones join; `dispatch` calls every handler, then the ack; an interrupt nobody claims is counted (`Unclaimed`) and the first one logged with the line's drivers; `String()` lists the lines. `e1000.Interrupt` returns whether ICR (read-to-clear) had a cause, and the driver registers on `env.Lines` (plain `Serve` where there is none, as in hostsim). `main` logs the table after the drivers start: `[driver] INFO: irq 11: e1000`, now a `boot_to_go` marker, the line taken from the PCI header as before. Host tests under `-race`: two fake devices on one `FakeIRQ`, 25 interrupts each, each served for its own and the line opened once; three unclaimed interrupts counted and logged once; the e1000 claims only with a cause set. W22's pitfalls are PIT-69 to PIT-75.

### [x] W22-S02 AC'97 and /dev/dsp (E18.F05)
`drivers/ac97`: probe PCI 8086:2415 with two I/O BARs; cold reset through the global control register, wait for the codec ready bit; unmute master and PCM-out volumes; VRA on, the rate set in the PCM front DAC rate register and read back; the PCM-out channel's buffer descriptor list (32 entries, each a DMA page of samples, interrupt on completion), the last valid index advanced as buffers fill, `ServeShared` on the device's line for completions. `kernel/sound` (pure, host-tested): a ring of buffers between writers and the driver, format conversion (U8 and mono to S16 stereo), the clock of what has been played (`GETODELAY`). `/dev/dsp` through devfs with `Ioctler` and `Poller`. `user/tone.c`: a 440 Hz sine for one second at 48000 Hz stereo, then 22050 Hz mono U8 for half a second, each reporting `GETOSPACE`. `make sound-smoke` (in `make test`): go.img with `-device AC97,audiodev=snd0 -audiodev wav,id=snd0,path=build/sound-smoke.wav`; `tests/smoke/checkwav.py` finds 440 Hz within 2 % for at least 0.9 s, then the second part, and silence around them. Run targets get `RUN_AUDIO ?=` (`-audiodev pa` under WSLg; `none` to disable). Markers: `[ac97] INFO: ac97 at io 0x..., codec ready, 48000 Hz (vra), irq N`.
As built: `drivers/ac97` (`regs.go`, `card.go`, `driver.go`): `pci.IOBase` and `pci.EnableIOBusMaster` are new; cold reset off, wait for the primary codec, mixer reset, master at full and PCM out at -12 dB, VRA on when the codec has it; the descriptor list and 32 buffers in DMA pages below 4 GiB; `Card` is `sound.Output`: `Write` stages frames into a page-sized buffer and hands each full one over (descriptor, then LVI; port I/O orders the two), keeping one descriptor free; `Drain` hands over a partial one and waits for the halt; after a halt (everything played) the next buffer resets the box and starts again from descriptor 0, and a halt that races the LVI write is caught by reading DCH after it; writers wait on a condition the interrupt broadcasts, with a 5 s stall limit (`ErrStalled`); `Interrupt` claims only when one of its status bits (BCIS, LVBCI, FIFOE) is set and clears them. `kernel/sound`: `format.go` converts U8 and mono to 16-bit stereo (whole frames, a partial one kept for the next write); `dsp.go` is `/dev/dsp` with one opener, OSS's defaults (8 kHz, U8, mono), `RESET`, `SYNC`, `POST`, `SPEED`, `SETFMT`, `CHANNELS`, `STEREO`, `GETFMTS`, `GETCAPS`, `SETFRAGMENT` (ignored), `GETBLKSIZE`, `GETOSPACE`, `GETODELAY`, a `Poller` for `POLLOUT`, and close draining. In QEMU the card is at I/O 0xc000/0xc400 on IRQ 11, which it shares with the e1000 (`[driver] INFO: irq 11: ac97, e1000`): S01 was needed. Tests: a fake AC'97 in Go (its registers, DMA pages by address, a bus master playing one descriptor per call) under `-race`: three rings in odd-sized writes come out complete and in order, a restart after a halt, a full ring blocking a writer until `Reset`; `kernel/sound`'s conversions and ioctls. `user/tone.c` is built against Linux's `<linux/soundcard.h>`, so a wrong request number or `audio_buf_info` would fail it. `make sound-smoke` (in `make test`): go.img with `-audiodev wav` and `-device AC97`; `tests/smoke/checkwav.py` measured 440 Hz for 1.0 s and 880 Hz for 0.5 s (QEMU writes 44100 Hz). The two tones meet with no silence (SYNC, then a new rate at once), so the checker labels 100 ms windows by pitch rather than splitting at silence. Interactive targets get the card with `RUN_AUDIO ?= none` (`RUN_AUDIO=pa` for PulseAudio under WSLg).

### [ ] W22-S03 Doom makes noise (E18.F05)

Not in this wave: music (Doom's MIDI through an OPL emulator), recording (PCM in), a mixer device (`/dev/mixer`) beyond fixed volumes, ALSA or PulseAudio, more than one program playing at once, Intel HDA, SB16 and ISA DMA, USB (and so a USB tablet), virtio-blk and virtio-net (the transport is ready for them), an I/O APIC or MSI (interrupts stay the 8259's).

Risks and pitfalls (PIT-NN entries go into BUGS_AND_MITIGATIONS.md when the wave starts):
- **Shared lines.** Two PCI devices on one 8259 line, a handler that claims an interrupt it did not raise, or one that claims none while the line stays asserted (level-triggered: an interrupt storm); every handler reads its own device's status, and an unclaimed interrupt is logged.
- **DMA and memory ordering.** The card reads buffers and descriptors the CPU just wrote, and virtio's used ring is written by the device: the order of writes before the doorbell (the last valid index, the notify register), and of the index before the data, matters, now on four CPUs; DMA pages are never Go heap (PIT-44).
- **Audio is real time; QEMU is not.** Under TCG the card drains at the virtual rate and a slow guest underruns; the ring must be deep enough, an underrun audible (a gap) rather than a hang, and the WAV checks look for the sound and its pitch, not for a perfect stream.
- **OSS is old and loose.** Programs ask for rates and formats the card cannot do and expect the nearest; `SNDCTL_DSP_*` numbers and `audio_buf_info` layouts must be Linux's exactly (checked against the host's `<sys/soundcard.h>`).
- **Absolute and relative do not mix.** X accelerates relative moves, mousedev's PS/2 stream is relative, and the screen's size can change (W21): converting a tablet's 0-32767 into moves needs the current mode, and X's acceleration off.
- **virtio's legacy and modern faces.** QEMU's transitional devices also offer the legacy I/O BAR; the driver uses only the modern capabilities, checks `VIRTIO_F_VERSION_1`, and fails loudly on a device without them.
- **Harness timing.** QMP's absolute events and the HMP monitor's keys are two sockets; keys files must wait for text or pixels between them, never sleep and hope.
