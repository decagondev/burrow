# W27 A Real PC's Devices  (M27)  — status: planned
Goal: Burrow runs on the devices a real PC has, not only QEMU's legacy ones, and one USB stick carries all of it. Demo: QEMU's `q35` machine with OVMF, no IDE controller and no PS/2 devices, boots `build/burrow-usb.img` from a USB stick (`usb-storage` on QEMU's xHCI): interrupts come through the I/O APIC and MSI; the root, home, X and data volumes are found by label on the stick, on a SATA disk (AHCI) and on an NVMe disk; a USB keyboard and mouse drive the console, Doom and the desktop; the same image written to a real stick boots a real PC far enough to report what it found (on the screen, W26-S04); `make test` green. Stories: E24.F01-F05 ([BACKLOG.md](../BACKLOG.md)).

Where we start (after W26): the firmware path works (UEFI or BIOS), but the devices Burrow drives after it are QEMU's `pc` machine's: interrupts through the 8259 in virtual-wire mode (`nucleus/irq.c`, ADR-0005), disks on the IDE controller (`drivers/ata`) or virtio, a PS/2 keyboard and mouse (`drivers/ps2`), an e1000 or virtio-net. Real PCs of the last decade have none of the first three: devices interrupt through the I/O APIC or by message (MSI, MSI-X), disks are SATA behind AHCI or NVMe, and keyboards and mice are USB, behind xHCI. A stick Burrow boots from is itself a USB disk, so without USB storage the kernel cannot mount the volumes on it. ACPI's MADT, which the nucleus already parses for CPUs (W20-S04), also names the I/O APICs and the interrupt overrides.

Design (each device proved on QEMU first; the old paths stay for `pc`):
- **Interrupts the modern way.** The nucleus programs the I/O APIC from the MADT (its address, the global system interrupts, the ISA overrides such as the timer on GSI 2), masks the 8259, and routes each line to a vector `irq_wait` already knows; PCI devices get MSI or MSI-X vectors (`pci.Capabilities`, a nucleus call to allocate a vector), legacy INTx through the ACPI `_PRT` only if a device has nothing else. ADR-0005 is unchanged: an interrupt is a wake-up for one Go goroutine.
- **Disks: AHCI and NVMe.** `drivers/ahci` (a port's command list and FIS in DMA pages, READ/WRITE DMA EXT, NCQ later) and `drivers/nvme` (one admin and one I/O queue pair, MSI-X) register `block.Writer`s, named `sd*` and `nvme0n1`, mounted by label like `hd*` and `vd*`; `block/blocktest` conformance for both, host tests against fake controllers like `virtio/virtiotest`.
- **USB: xHCI, then HID and storage.** `kernel/usb` (pure: descriptors, the hub and port state machine, device enumeration, control/bulk/interrupt transfer requests) over `drivers/xhci` (rings in DMA pages, the event ring on an MSI vector, root ports); class drivers on top: HID boot protocol keyboards and mice into `evdev` and the console as the PS/2 ones feed them, and mass storage (bulk-only transport, SCSI READ(10)/WRITE(10)/READ CAPACITY) as `block.Writer`s named `ub*`. Hot-plug is enumeration on a port change; unplugging a mounted disk ends its I/O with errors, not a panic.
- **One stick holds everything.** `make uefi-usb` (W26-S05) already puts the volumes on one GPT disk; the kernel now reads partitions (GPT, and MBR for old data disks) on every block device and mounts them by label wherever they are.

### [ ] W27-S01 The I/O APIC and MSI (E24.F01)
The nucleus finds the I/O APICs and overrides in the MADT (`acpi.c`), masks the 8259 and routes the ISA lines it uses (the PIT or LAPIC timer, keyboard, mouse, serial, RTC, the IDE lines) through the I/O APIC, falling back to virtual wire when the MADT has none; PCI MSI and MSI-X (`kernel/pci` capabilities, `nu_irq_alloc_msi`), used first by virtio and the e1000. ktests for the routing on `pc` and `q35`; `make test` unchanged on `pc`, and a `q35` boot of go.img in `make q35-smoke`.

### [ ] W27-S02 AHCI and NVMe (E24.F02)
`drivers/ahci` and `drivers/nvme`, host-tested against fakes and `blocktest`; `disk-smoke` and `dev-smoke` halves with the data disk on `-device ahci` and `-device nvme`; `make disk-bench` gains both. Partition tables read on every block device (`kernel/block/part.go`: GPT with its CRCs, MBR), each partition a `block.Device` (`sda1`, `nvme0n1p1`) mounted by label.

### [ ] W27-S03 xHCI and USB keyboards and mice (E24.F03)
`drivers/xhci` and `kernel/usb` (enumeration, hubs, control and interrupt transfers), HID boot keyboards and mice into `evdev` and the console; `make usb-smoke` boots `q35` without PS/2 (`-device qemu-xhci -device usb-kbd -device usb-mouse`) and types the go-smoke session, then drives paint and X with the mouse; keys files work unchanged (QEMU's `sendkey` reaches the USB keyboard).

### [ ] W27-S04 USB storage (E24.F04)
Bulk-only mass storage over `kernel/usb` (`ub*` devices, SCSI commands, a stall or reset recovered, a removed stick failing its I/O); `make usb-smoke` also mounts a data disk on `-device usb-storage` and runs dev-smoke's edit, compile and reboot on it.

### [ ] W27-S05 One stick, on q35 and on a real PC (E24.F05)
`build/burrow-usb.img` boots on `q35` + OVMF from `usb-storage` alone: the root, home, X and net volumes on the stick, the desktop as burrow (the unix-desktop demo), `make usb-desktop-smoke`. A first-boot report on the screen and in `/var/log/hw.txt` (what the kernel found: CPUs, the interrupt controller, every PCI device with its driver or none, the disks and partitions, the USB devices), so a tester sends one photo or one file. `make usb-initrd` writes a smaller image that needs no disk driver at all (the console, busybox and Doom in the initrd) for a first "does it boot" try. REAL_HARDWARE.md updated: what to try first, what to send back.

### [ ] W27-S06 M27
`make q35-smoke`, `usb-smoke` and `usb-desktop-smoke` in `make test` on one CPU and four; `disk-bench` with AHCI and NVMe beside IDE and virtio; ARCHITECTURE.md §7.12 (interrupt routing, the USB stack's layers), ADR-0014 (the I/O APIC and MSI beside virtual wire; one USB stack for HID and storage); RISKS.md R-06 re-scored with what real machines reported; the DEVLOG wave entry.

Exit: **M27 demo — a real PC's devices.** On `q35` with OVMF and no legacy devices, Burrow boots from a USB stick and runs the console, Doom and the desktop with a USB keyboard and mouse, volumes on USB, SATA and NVMe, interrupts through the I/O APIC and MSI; `pc` boots as before; the stick image and the initrd-only image are written for a real PC with a guide to what to report; `make test` green on both CPU counts; DEVLOG wave entry.

Not in this wave: Intel HD Audio, real network cards (Intel e1000e/igc, Realtek r8169) and USB storage as a boot device for the BIOS path (a possible W28, for a particular machine's report), Wi-Fi, a GPU driver (GOP's framebuffer stays), USB 2 controllers (EHCI) and USB hubs beyond what QEMU and xHCI's root hub need, suspend, batteries and laptop extras, ACPI AML evaluation (unless a real machine's power-off needs it: then W28).

Risks and pitfalls (PIT-NN entries go into BUGS_AND_MITIGATIONS.md when the wave starts):
- **Interrupt routing differs per machine.** ISA overrides, active-low and level-triggered lines, PCI pins behind bridges; QEMU's `q35` covers the common case only. MSI first everywhere it exists, and the routing table printed at boot so a real machine's report shows it.
- **DMA and caches on real controllers.** QEMU forgives a missing barrier or a buffer above 4 GiB that a 32-bit-only controller cannot reach; DMA pages come from below 4 GiB unless the controller says it can do 64 bits.
- **xHCI is a big specification.** Rings, cycle bits, contexts, port resets and timeouts; build only what HID and storage need, host-test the pure parts, and stop on the first controller error with its state printed.
- **USB timing is real time.** Port resets and device settle times are milliseconds the Go kernel must actually wait; QEMU is lenient, real devices are not.
- **A real PC is tested by someone else.** No serial port, no debugger: the screen report (W26-S04, S05 here) is the only evidence, so every failure must say where it stopped.
